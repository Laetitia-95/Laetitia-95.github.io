## Project Overview

As part of the "Facility Logistics Management and Analytics" class, this group project evaluated **a facility located in San Marcos, California** through an on-site facility tour and structured warehouse assessment. The objective was to observe operations, evaluate performance across multiple operational areas, and identify opportunities for improvement. 

The assessment examined **11 areas of warehouse performance**, including customer satisfaction, safety and ergonomics, space utilization, material handling, workforce management, storage and inventory, order picking, supply chain coordination, information technology, quality, and operational efficiency and flexibility. 

The final score of **51/121** highlighted both effective visible operations and opportunities to improve processes, systems, and performance management. 

## Facility Overview

The assessed facility manufactured and distributed irrigation and outdoor lighting products, including sprinklers, rotors, nozzles, valves, controllers, sensors, and LED lighting systems. The San Marcos site included manufacturing, distribution, and office operations and supported a large number of finished goods and raw materials. 

The warehouse operated under a **make-to-stock strategy**, with finished goods stored in the distribution center before customer orders are received. The warehouse activities included receiving, put-away, storage, order picking, and shipping. It manages around **90,000 SKU variations**. 

## Assessment Methodology

The facility was evaluated using a **Single-Tour Warehouse Assessment** covering 11 areas. Each area was evaluated using observations from the facility tour, discussions with management, and a structured assessment framework. Scores were assigned from **1 to 11** with 1 being "Poor", 3 "Below Average", 5 "Average", 7 "Above Average", 9 "Excellent" and 11 "Best in Class". 

Because the assessment was based on a single facility tour, the results represent a diagnostic assessment rather than a complete performance audit. Some activities were not operating during the visit, and detailed operational data such as order accuracy, inventory accuracy, picking travel time, and equipment utilization were not available.

## Assessment Results

The warehouse received an overall score of **51/121**. The strongest areas were **Cleanliness, Ergonomics, Safety and Hygiene** and **Material Handling Equipment Condition** with a score of **7/11**. The facility was overall clean and organized, with pedestrian walkways, safety mirros, appropriate material handling equipment, and an internal safety program. 

The lowest rates were given to **Supply Chain Coordination, Level and Use of IT, Commitment to Quality, and Managing Efficiency and Flexibility** areas, each receiving **3/11**. These areas were affected by reliance on manual processes and employee experience, limited formal performance measurement, inconsistent process standardization, and limited real-time information. 

### Storage and Inventory Management
The warehouse used several storage approaches, including a **forward pick area for fast-moving products, fixed lower-level pick locations, shared storage, and two- and three-deep storage** to increase storage density. With around 90,000 SKUS, these approaches create a complex replenishment environment. Shared storage and changing replenishment locations can also reduce inventory visibility and create situations where multiple SKUs are present around the same picking location. 

### Order Picking and Workforce
The company chose to use **S-shape path** for order picking. However, the existing WMS did not provide optimized pick paths or slotting recommendations, leaving employees to determine many picking decisions based on experience. This reliance on employee knowledge was important because the company also used temporary workers and informal on-the-job training. Both can create variation in picking methods and performance between experienced and newer employees.  

### Information Technology and Performance Visibility
The warehouse relied on a WMS with limited support for operational decision-making. Performance measures such as picks per employee and inbound/outbound throughput were tracked manually, while real-time operational visibility and formal performance monitoring remained limited. The company was transitioning to **Oracle Fusion**, creating an opportunity to introduce greater system guidance, inventory visibility, directed picking, and formal KPI tracking.  

### Layout, Flow, and Quality
Multiple layout constraints were observed, including limited staging space, receiving and shipping activities sharing the same general dock area, quality inspection located away from receiving, and limited signage identifying warehouse activity areas. These conditions can increase travel, congestion, and process complexity. Picking discrepancies, particularly incorrect quantities and incorrect items, were also identified during discussions with management, while formal accuracy and quality KPIs were limited. 

## Recommendations

Based on the assessment, four major improvement areas were identified: 
1. **Improve cross-functional coordination and process alignment**: 
 - Separate raw materials and finished goods more clearly
 - Establish standardized handoffs between receiving, quality, replenishment, and picking
 - Develop visual workflows to improve coordination across operations

2. **Accelerate IT modernization and reduce reliance on manual decision-making**: use the transition to Oracle Fusion as an opportunity to introduce directed picking, automated replenishment logic, real-time dashboards, and more consistent system-driven processes. 

3. **Strengthen quality control and accuracy monitoring**: 
 - Establish inspection zones closer to receiving
 - Introduce accuracy-focused KPIs
 - Communicate performance through dashboards or daily briefs
 - Use audits and root-cause analysis to address recurring picking errors 

4. **Improve layout, staging, and equipment organization**: 
 - Create dedicated inbound and outbound docks
 - Imporve equipment storage
 - Review warehouse travel paths and activity locations to reduce congestion and process interference

## Key Findings

The assessment showed an important contrast between the warehouse's visible operating condition and its underlying processes. The facility appeared clean, organized, and capable of supporting daily operations, but discussions with management and the tour revealed greater relaince on manual processes, employee experience, and limited system support than was initially visible. 

The warehouse showed strenghts in **safety, cleanliness, material handling equipment, and its ability to manage a large SKU assorment**, while the most significant improvement opportunities involved **IT integration, quality and performance measurement, process standardization, and cross-functional coordination**. 

This project demonstrated how a structured facility assessment can combine direct observation, operational concepts, and performance criteria to diagose warehouse operations and develop targeted improvement recommendations.
