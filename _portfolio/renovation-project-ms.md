## Project Overview

As part of the "Advanced Project Management" class, this group project developed a project management plan for a proposed sustainability renovation of an academic building at California State University San Marcos (CSUSM). 

The project focused on improving the building's energy efficiency through upgrades to **insulation, windows, HVAC systems, lighting, and air sealing** while meeting California Title 24 and CSU standards. The proposed project was planned over approximately **two years** with an estimated budget of **$24-25 million**. It applied project management concepts across the full project lifecycle, including **scope, stakeholder communication, scheduling, resources, cost, risk, quality, supply chain, and project closeout**. Microsot Project was used to develop the detailed schedule, assign resources, establish dependencies, and identify the critical path.  

## Project Scope and Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)

The **project scope** defined the renovation boundaries, major deliverables, assumptions, constraints, and acceptance criteria. The proposed improvements included upgrades to the building envelope, HVAC systems, windows, lighting, and air sealing without expanding the existing building. 

A **WBS** was developed to organize the project into eight major phases: 
1. Project Management
2. Building Assessment
3. Design
4. Procurement
5. Permits and Approvals
6. Execution
7. Commissioning and Verification
8. Closeout

Each phase was further divided into work packages and activities, creating the foundation for scheduling, resource assignment, and budget planning.

## Stakeholder and Communication Management

A stakeholder analysis was developed to identify and prioritize the parties affected by or involved in the project. Stakeholders included the project sponsor, project manager, facilities management, contractors and suppliers, faculty and students, permitting authorities, and other university organizations. The stakeholders were evaluated according to factors such as **power, interest, influence, impact, urgency, and legitimacy**, allowing communication and engagement strategies to be adapted to their roles and level of involvement. 

The communication plan established the appropriate communication method and frequency for different stakeholder groups, ranging from regular project meetings and progress reports to milestone walkthroughs and notifications of campus disruptions. 

## Project Scheduling with Microsoft Project

The detailed project schedule was developed using **Microsoft Project**. Activities from the WBS were sequenced using task dependencies, assigned estimated durations and resources, and organized into a project schedule. 

The plan ran from **March 31, 2026 to February 25, 2028** with major milestones including project charter approval, design approval, contract award, renovation completion, and occupance authorization.

![Gantt Chart]({{site.baseurl}}/images/gantt-chart-project.png)

MS Project was also used to generate the **Gantt chart and identify the critical path**. Critical activities with zero slack were identified because any delays to these activities could directly affect the overall project completion date. 

![Critical Path]({{site.baseurl}}/images/critical-path-project.png)

## Budget and Resource Planning

Project resources included the project management team, designers and engineers, contractors, facilities personnel, equipment, and materials. Resources were assigned to scheduled activities, while a **RACI matrix** was developed to clarify responsibility, accountability, consultation, and communication across major work packages.  

![RACI Matrix]({{site.baseurl}}/images/raci-matrix-project.png)

The project budget incorporated estimated **labor, materials, equipment, permits, certifications, and contingency reserves**. Cost estimates were developed using publicly available wage and construction-cost references and were treated as planning estimates prior to formal supplier quotations and proposals. 

## Risk, Quality, and Supply Chain Planning

A risk management plan was developed to identify potential threats to schedule, cost, quality, safety, and campus operations. The major risks included permitting delays, supply chain disruptions, cost overruns, construction disruption, safety incidents, and compliance issues. The mitigation strategies included early permit submissions, contingency planning, alternative suppliers, early procurement, and ongoing monitoring. 

The quality planning focused on ensuring that project deliverables met Title 24, CSU, and defined performance requirements through design reviews, inspections, testing, commissioning, and verification. 

The supply chain planning was important for long-lead materials and equipment. The plan incorporated early procurement, pre-qualified vendors, alternate suppliers, and coordination between design, procurement, and construction activities to reduce the risk of material-related schedule delays. 

## Key Findings

This project showed how the different components of project management must work together rather than being managed independently. **Scope and WBS provided the foundation for scheduling, while the schedule influenced resource allocation, budgeting, procurement, risk management, and stakeholder coordination**. The use of **Microsoft Project** provided experience translating a project into a structured sxchedule with activities, dependencies, milestones, assigned resources, and critical path analysis. 

The project provided experience developing an integrated project management plan from **projec tinitiation and planning through execution planning, monitoring and closeout**.
